### 4. 1. Asking for a draft

Copy this prompt, then add the specified source material:

```text
Write a 300-500 character notice for new students
about AI use in this course. Include that AI is allowed,
that students must check the result,
and that they keep a short AI-use record on GitHub.
```

Paste the supplied brief below. Ask AI to use only supplied course URLs and facts.

### 5. Writing actions a reader can follow

Abstract instructions

Use AI effectively.  
Verify it appropriately.  
Create high-quality work.

A concrete action

If AI's answer has a number,  
find it in the original source.  
Record where you checked it.

### 6. 2. Editing the draft yourself

01

Break up long explanations.

Order the text by the actions your reader takes first.

02

Replace vague verbs with specific actions.

For example, replace verify with compare the numbers in the source.

03

Read it from your reader's perspective.

Will they know what to open and what to check?

### 7. A one-page guide

Use one heading.  
Put the required actions in order.  
Shorten the text if the letters get too small.  
  
Use your usual document or slide software.

A text-only submission is fine. A separate design-tool account is unnecessary.

### 8. An optional illustration with a purpose

Copy this prompt, then add the specified source material:

```text
Create an illustration for this notice.
Show someone opening a document to check it.
Use no real students or university logos.
Leave space for the notice text.
```

Image generation is optional. Text and simple shapes are enough.

### 9. Checking an illustration

01

Check that it supports the notice's meaning.

Does it help readers understand the required action?

02

Check any text and small details.

Look for spelling errors, unwanted logos and misleading details.

03

Request a correction only where needed.

Add a description so the meaning is available to people who cannot see it.

### 10. A 30-second reader check

Did the reader understand the purpose of the notice?

Did they understand the first action to take?

Did they understand what to record?

Did they identify one unfamiliar word?

If no other reader is available, take a break and read it again yourself.

## Save and submit

Save the artifact and a short AI-use record in `week06/README.md`, then commit all required files. Submit the **final commit URL** through the course form. Initial registration uses the repository URL; weekly and final submissions use a commit URL.

Do not include student IDs, university emails, passwords, or confidential source material. Full AI chat histories are not required. Self-study readers can complete the exercise without university forms.

Character limits in prompts follow the source exercise. Follow any language-specific length specified by your instructor.
