# Using the material at your university

This is an introductory set of 14 lessons and a final project. You may use selected lessons. Japanese PowerPoint decks contain teaching notes; slide-only PDFs are suitable for students. English lesson notes and setup instructions are in `en/`.

## Prepare your course information

| Item | What to provide or change |
|---|---|
| Course page | The LMS or website students should open |
| Accounts and AI | Required sign-in account and AI services available in your course |
| Student template | An empty repository with setup, week01–week14, and final folders |
| Instructor invitation | Your GitHub username in setup slide 27 and the setup guide |
| Student numbers | Your university's format in slide 29 and the registration instructions |
| Submission | Initial registration, weekly work, and final-project destinations |
| Feedback | Where and when students receive checking results |
| Assessment | Your course deadlines and published assessment policy |

`tsuchiyatakahirolab` is the material author's account shown as an example. Replace the invitation username with the course instructor's account. `2026GT0001` is an example student-number format, not a rule for every university.

## Set up the student workflow

Use the [public student template](https://github.com/tsuchiyatakahirolab/ai-data-literacy-student-template) or the empty `student-template/` folder. If you adapt it, place the empty template in a repository you manage and share that URL. Students use Use this template → Create a new repository to create their own Private `ai-learning`. Existing participants keep their repository.

Initial setup saves one image and a short record in `setup/`, invites the specified instructor, and registers the repository URL. Weekly submissions use the final commit URL after saving all required work and a short AI-use record. Full AI conversations and paid plans are not required.

Provide the form or LMS used by your course, its actual fields, and the account students should use. Keep student identities and submissions out of public repositories. Tell students how their registration, access, image, and record will be checked and where to find feedback. Late joiners use the same setup guide; communicate past-assignment deadlines separately.

## Choose and adapt the materials

Edit the PPTX for teaching and use PDFs for student viewing. Use the handouts for copyable prompts. The PDFs preserve the slide appearance as images; editable and copyable text is available in the PPTX and handouts. Independent learners can follow the [self-study guide](self_study.md) without course registration or instructor invitations.
